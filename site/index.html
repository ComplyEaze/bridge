A thick case file is followed through eight short chapters, as a 3D book where the browser can draw one. It arrives with twenty-six sticky index flags down its edge, coloured by the kind of finding (flagging findings is in development and not in the download). Asked who owes money for more than 90 days, the flags fold back into the edge, it opens to an ageing page, and the five overdue parties are ringed in pencil. The page turns to a voucher, two pencil ticks are drawn, and its 40A(3) flag leaves the page and lands as row 1 of a working paper titled “Working paper · Tax audit FY 2025-26” (flagging is in development and not in the download). The page turns again to a bank statement; a slip lifts off each of five rows and the pile reads 139 matched, and three question-mark flags mark near-miss ledger names that wait for your decision; you decide them one after another, each turns into a tick, and the count of lines matched to your ledgers rises from 139 to 142; ComplyEaze Bridge then prepares one receipt as its own one-voucher file, and the other 141 vouchers as a file for you to import in Tally. A replica of the approval window (the text of release 0.4.2, in the frame of release 0.3.0 on a Mac; release 0.5.0 words it differently) rises from that receipt's row, asks you to post that one voucher, and shrinks back into the row with a red tick: one voucher per approval, and a voucher is posted this way or imported in a file, never both. After you import the file of the other 141 in Tally yourself, each voucher is ticked as ComplyEaze Bridge reads it back, counting up to 142 of 142: 141 imported and one posted. A narrow receipt slip slides out for the calls made. Finally the flags retract, the file shuts on one tick, and the five install steps and the download follow.

At a glance, release 0.6.1

  • You can ask: who owes you and whom you owe, with ageing of the open bills; the trial balance for a period; the vouchers in a date range, found by number, reference, narration or amount and added up by ledger, month, voucher type, group or primary group (checked on one synthetic book, not a large one); Tally’s own Cash Flow for whole months, shown only when its net total ties to the trial balance (not a cash flow statement under AS 3; tried on two synthetic books); one ledger’s opening, debits, credits and closing; and Tally’s own month-wise Sales or Purchase Register for whole months (not tied to the vouchers; read on one synthetic book); Tally’s own Negative Stock and Negative Ledgers lists for a window (being listed is not a finding; synthetic books only); and the vouchers of one type that Tally marks Uncertain in its GSTR-1 (a Sales type, measured on one synthetic book).
  • Partly, with limits: profit and loss and the balance sheet, which on a book with stock items are read only when Tally’s own closing stock closes every gap (one synthetic book measured) and which a book with several currencies does refuse; closing stock value per item at 31 March and its closing quantity where Tally’s own Stock Summary shows the item on a line of its own, on small books only, and only the year to 31 March 2026 has been checked; and a password-protected PDF statement from the banks the Download page lists, read into a file of Payment, Receipt and Contra vouchers that you check and import in Tally yourself. It creates no ledger, it cannot build onto a Cash-in-Hand ledger, a line it cannot place goes to a suspense ledger you name, and it has not yet been run on a real bank statement.
  • Not in release 0.6.1: GST returns or GSTR-2B matching; making sales or purchase invoices or GST entries; tax-audit flags (one test, the cash share of receipts and payments against the s.44AB(a) limit, is off by default and not yet run against a live TallyPrime); Tally’s Funds Flow and Ratio Analysis reports.
  • “You can ask” means it answered on the books and builds we ran it on, which the README lists (not every read has its own recorded run); the program in the published Windows 0.6.1 file has had one read-only run against TallyPrime 7.2 Silver, on one Windows computer and one synthetic company on 27 October 2026, started by our test client, not from Claude Desktop (it read Tally’s status, listed the companies and read the ledger masters; nothing was posted); the Mac file has not been run against TallyPrime. Very large books can fail or take longer than the assistant waits, and a book with several currencies is read only in part. What it reads goes to your AI provider (where your client’s data goes). Set it up in five steps.

  1. In TallyPrime, press F1, then Settings, then Connectivity, then Client/Server configuration. Set “TallyPrime acts as” to Both, note the Port number (usually 9000), save, then close and reopen TallyPrime.
  2. In Claude Desktop, open Settings, then Extensions, then Advanced settings, and click Install Extension. Choose the downloaded .mcpb file. In our test, Claude Desktop showed its own notice that the extension is not verified by Anthropic, then asked us to confirm.
  3. Read the Terms of Use linked in the settings that open (if none open, open Settings, then Extensions, then ComplyEaze Bridge), then tick “I accept the ComplyEaze Bridge Terms of Use”. Until you do, ComplyEaze Bridge does not read from Tally.
  4. Type your Tally port (usually 9000). Leave the Tally host as localhost and leave posting off.
  5. Click Save, then quit Claude Desktop completely and open it again. Ask “Which company is open in Tally?” Claude Desktop may ask your permission before it uses a tool; in our test we chose Allow once.
