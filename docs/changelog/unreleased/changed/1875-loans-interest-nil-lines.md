- The tax audit's loans and interest test now reads a ledger on a nil (zero-amount) line as no posting in three more places, as it already did for the loan rows. A loan entry credited from an expense ledger beside a nil bank line is now asked about as the lender's charge or a loan taken, and counted among the expense entries; its clause 31 row's title says so in place of the running-balance flag, which stays in its limits. In the other direction, an entry is no longer asked about as a lender's charge where only a nil line made it look like one: against another loan's interest ledger, it is the misposted-interest question instead, and where every other line is nil, it is neither. A TDS or interest ledger only on a nil line is no longer named among the loan's interest TDS ledgers or the misposted-interest finding's ledgers. Interest booked beside a nil line on its loan is no longer hidden from the check that its ledger is declared shared. These are declared differences from the reference engine (#1875).
