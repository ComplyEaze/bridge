- After a request to Tally is cut at its time limit, or otherwise ends without a whole answer, ComplyEaze Bridge now refuses its next request to that Tally, from any of its processes, until Tally has finished: Tally goes on working on a cut request, and a request sent meanwhile only waits behind it and is cut too. The next request first sends `GET /status`, each with its own 5-second limit, and goes ahead once three in a row have answered, 2 seconds apart, whatever their answer; a status check cut by its limit, or with no answer at all, starts the count again. Until then the request is not sent and is refused as `tally_endpoint_draining`, with `retry_after_s`; it clears by itself 10 minutes after the cut request was sent, whatever the status checks did. A computer whose status checks always take more than a second, but answer, is held for up to two refused calls; that has not been measured. A request that Tally answered with an error status, or with a reply Bridge refuses (another encoding, or a length over its limit), holds nothing: Tally had finished it. A post to the books is refused the same way before its attempt is recorded. Two lab rows on one synthetic book, with one long read cut at 20 seconds, measured that `/status` waits while Tally works and that a read sent after three quick status checks answered at its usual speed. A third row ran it through ComplyEaze Bridge's own build, twice: the request sent straight after a cut was refused as draining, and the next, sent after `retry_after_s`, went ahead after three status checks and answered whole. A request let through after a drain takes about 4 seconds longer than usual, for the pauses between those checks (#2142).
