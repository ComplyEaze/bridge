# What a ledger's `Eligible for Input Tax Credit` setting looks like on the wire: one ledger created, its setting keyed Yes and then No on the screen, each followed by a read, on BRIDGE SHAPE LAB

Synthetic company `BRIDGE SHAPE LAB` only, loaded alone, nobody else using this PC for Tally or this book from step 0 to step 11. **This sheet WRITES, on the screen and nowhere else:** it CREATES ONE new ledger named exactly `ITC Test Ledger` (under `Purchase Accounts`, no opening balance) and then ALTERS that one ledger twice (its GST details history, steps 6 and 7). Nothing else is keyed, altered, accepted, created or deleted; no existing master is opened for a change; no voucher screen is opened; Tally's current date is never changed; never use Accept As Is. A backup is taken first (to a new empty local folder) and the book is restored from it at the end, which is expected to remove the created ledger (a created stock item was removed by a restore on this book on 15 Oct, verified there): no master is ever deleted, by hand or over the gateway, and the first reads are compared with the reads after the restore. Nothing is sent over the gateway except reads: 13 sends of three request files, all Export (listed in full in the appendix). **The sitting is LIVE from step 5 on: the operator at the lab PC and the coordinator in the same thread, one instruction at a time.** **Every key pressed in Tally from step 5 on (Yes, No, Escape, Ctrl+A, Ctrl+Q included) is pressed only when the coordinator has written it in the thread.** The only things done without an instruction are screenshots, the sends and `check.py` runs of the steps, and, at the looks of steps 6 and 7, the Up and Down arrow keys and the few Enter presses that step 6(a) lists. After each instruction the operator posts a screenshot and waits. `RESTORE`, when the coordinator writes it, is the go-ahead for step 9 as a whole; each Tally key of step 9 is still written one at a time. **The counted write grant for this sheet is: 1 backup, 1 ledger create, 2 ledger alters (setting Yes, then No, each followed by one wire read), 1 restore proven equal to the first reads (identical, or equal leaf by leaf with only the header counters differing); nothing else, no other company.**

Why, and what this is evidence of. The element `GSTINELIGIBLEITC` of a GST details entry looks like an INELIGIBILITY flag. On a stock item of this book the screen `Eligible for Input Tax Credit` read No where the wire had Yes, and read Yes where the element was absent (one item, two rows). The shipped ledger read maps Yes to `itc_eligible: true`, and no ledger has ever been read with its screen setting beside the wire value. This run keys the ledger's setting on the screen, Yes and then No, and reads the wire after each. Only step 7 changes ONE thing (the setting, Yes to No); step 6 keys a whole row and the setting together.

**What the outcome means (decided before the sitting).** W6 and W7 are the wire state of `GSTINELIGIBLEITC` (Yes / No / absent) on the keyed entry after step 6 (screen Yes) and after step 7 (screen No). W6 absent and W7 Yes, or W6 No and W7 Yes -> the element is an ineligibility flag on a ledger (Yes where the screen says No). W6 Yes and W7 absent or No -> it is an eligibility flag (Yes where the screen says Yes). The setting counts only if its label, as photographed at the sitting, reads exactly `Eligible for Input Tax Credit` letter by letter; any other label (a negated one such as `Ineligible ...`, or a history column headed `Input Tax Credit`) is NOT SETTLED, with the label recorded as the finding. W6 is the wire state printed on the table line of the keyed entry (the `--expect-entry` date) in the step-6 `led` output, and W7 the same at step 7, whatever arrow or `new since` line is printed beside them. At step 6 only, whatever else the lines or notes print (Tally's own entry being edited, a re-dated entry, ledger-level leaves Tally adds at a first save) does not unsettle the run. Every other pair, an unmoved element, a setting not shown, any exit other than 0 of `check.py` at step 7, any `also moved:` or `moved:` on the step-7 `led` line, or a further field that appeared only when No was set -> NOT SETTLED, and nothing is rectified from this run. NOT SETTLED also covers a setting stored outside the fields this read fetches: an unmoved element then cannot be told from a value kept elsewhere. One book, one release, one ledger kind: every reading is PARTIAL.
