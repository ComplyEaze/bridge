# Windows item 628-C0b v2: a second copy of one supplier invoice, an optional copy, a cancelled copy, a Journal and a Payment to the same supplier, keyed on Tally's screen, then read through ComplyEaze Bridge to learn what a check for an invoice already in the books can see. (26 Oct 2026; v2 replaces v1: its hand-written reads are gone, and every voucher read is a call to the lab's ComplyEaze Bridge build. v1's comparison of a narrower read is parked until that read exists as a reviewed request builder.) SYNTHETIC ONLY: THE COMPANY is `BRIDGE GST LEDGER LAB`, the company GS1-L1 created (its GUID as GS1-L1 step 1 recorded it). Runs only after item 628-C0 v1.2 has closed, in the same sitting or a later one; a FINDING in C0 does not stop this item, a STOP in C0 does. LAB-COMMON v3 applies in full; where an item and LAB-COMMON differ, the stricter holds.

Why: issue 1904 asks ComplyEaze Bridge to refuse a purchase whose supplier invoice is already in the books, so input tax credit is never claimed twice. Its design reads a supplier's vouchers for a whole year and compares the supplier's invoice reference, the voucher number and the bill names. This item keys, on Tally's own screen, the copies such a check must find or deliberately leave aside, and records what Bridge's own voucher read returns for them: (1) whether a Journal and a Payment carry the supplier invoice reference and the party; (2) what an optional and a cancelled copy keep; (3) whether the voucher's party GSTIN is filled on a keyed voucher; (4) what Tally 7.2 shows when a second copy of a supplier invoice number is keyed (protocol reference §9.19 records that Tally saved one, but its screen was read only after the save, so whether it warned is not recorded; this was item C2's question in issue 628's sequence); (5) whether a keyed Purchase's bill allocation carries its name in Bridge's read (§9.19 recorded an export with an amount and no name); (6) the bytes Bridge's read costs per voucher over December and over the year.
